San Diego, California

Adopting AI in a county office is a trust problem before it is a technology problem.

Districts will not follow guidance they had no hand in writing. Families will not accept a tool they cannot ask questions about. Staff will not change practice because a memo told them to. The work is convening, explaining, and writing down what we agreed to, and then being honest about what the technology costs and where it fails.

Who I am

I am a bilingual program leader at the San Diego County Office of Education, currently Senior Program Specialist for Early Education Data Reporting. I convene school districts and community based early learning providers across the county, deliver professional learning to more than 500 staff a year, and build the reporting systems underneath a program that spans roughly 360 partner agencies.

Before that I was a digital forensics specialist for the California Department of Justice, and before that a systems technician here at SDCOE. I hold an M.S. in Cyber Security and Operations Leadership. That order matters. I came to AI through security and student data privacy rather than the other way around, and it shapes everything on this site.

Why this site exists

I built it as part of my application for the Coordinator, Artificial Intelligence position at SDCOE. It is a personal site. Nothing here is an SDCOE publication, and nothing here draws on non public information.

What I actually do, in specifics

  • Convene groups that do not report to meI plan and facilitate countywide data representative meetings for all SDQPI participants, districts and non-LEA early learning and care providers alike, in person and over Zoom, using live polls, guided recaps, and participatory discussion.
  • Teach mixed roomsProfessional learning for more than 500 staff annually, from program secretaries and clerks to systems technicians and analysts, on QRIS, SEIS, CALPADS, and other state and federal reporting systems.
  • Communicate in two languagesBilingual English and Spanish program communications: reporting timelines, stakeholder surveys, and data visuals that guide how hundreds of agencies collect and submit information.
  • Use AI on my own work, with reviewGenerative AI with human-in-the-loop review to build reporting automation, quality assurance pipelines, and staff facing tools. The review step is the part I take seriously.
  • Build the plumbingPython ETL pipelines and reporting applications that remove an estimated 120 hours of manual processing per quarter, plus Power Automate flows, Power Apps intake forms, and Power BI and SharePoint integrations.
  • Answer for the dataFERPA, COPPA and CIPA, confidentiality, audit, and records retention requirements across the systems I run, grounded in a cybersecurity master's and two years of forensic casework.
  • Serve on the bodies that decide thingsThe cross divisional committee that co-created the county office 2025-30 strategic plan, and the Human Resources committee revising evaluation processes for classified and certificated management.

Where the work sits

California has issued the guidance. The classroom is where it has to land. The middle layer is the part nobody has automated, and it is the part this role occupies.
