A proposal, based on public information only

What I would do in the first 90 days, and what I would refuse to rush.

A new coordinator arrives under pressure to produce something visible fast. The trap is publishing guidance in month one that nobody helped write, then spending a year explaining why districts ignored it. This plan spends the first month listening on purpose.

About this page

Type
Proposal
Basis
Public information only
Status
Not adopted by anyone
Structure
Three phases across 90 days

Days 1 to 30

Listen, and find out what is already true

Assume the county is further along than any document says. Somewhere there is a district that already wrote a policy, a teacher running an AI club, a principal who banned it, and a parent group that is worried. Find them before writing anything.

  • Meet the existing task force and working groups, read every prior artifact, and understand what has already been promised to whom.
  • Interview a deliberately uneven sample: a large unified district, two small districts, a charter network, a juvenile court and community schools site, and at least two people whose job is not technology.
  • Run a short countywide readiness survey. Ask what tools are already in use, what is already prohibited, what people are afraid of, and what they want help with first. Publish the results back to the people who answered.
  • Meet internally across divisions, especially cybersecurity, legal and privacy, purchasing, communications, human resources, and the teams already delivering AI professional learning.
  • Read the CDE guidance and model policy line by line and mark the decisions each district still has to make locally.

What exists at day 30: a written landscape summary, a stakeholder map, survey results with response rates by district size, and a short list of the three problems people named most often.

Why the survey goes out first

It does two jobs. It gives me a baseline I can measure against in a year, and it tells several hundred people that the new coordinator asked before telling. That second job is worth more.

Days 31 to 60

Frame the work and draft in the open

  • Bring the task force a proposed charter: what the group decides, what it advises on, who it reports to, how often it meets, and how it will make decisions when members disagree. Governance bodies fail on ambiguity more than on disagreement.
  • Draft the first two artifacts districts asked for. Based on what county offices are usually asked for, that is likely a local policy adaptation guide keyed to the CDE model policy, and a tool review checklist covering SOPIPA and AB 1584 contract terms, data retention, and training use.
  • Circulate drafts as drafts, with a comment deadline and named credit for contributors. Guidance written in public gets adopted. Guidance revealed finished gets forwarded and forgotten.
  • Design the professional learning sequence rather than a one off workshop. A first session for staff, a leadership session for cabinets and boards, and a family session in English and Spanish.
  • Agree on the measures now, before there is anything to defend: reach, adoption, satisfaction, and at least one measure of harm avoided or risk reduced.

What exists at day 60: a task force charter, two circulating drafts with named contributors, a professional learning calendar, and an agreed measurement plan.

Days 61 to 90

Publish, deliver, and pilot something small

  • Publish version one of both artifacts, dated and versioned, with a stated review cycle. Guidance without a version number and a review date is guidance nobody will trust in eighteen months.
  • Deliver the first professional learning sessions, including one family session in Spanish that is not a translated slide deck but a session designed for that audience.
  • Run one internal pilot with real measurement, chosen because it is boring and useful. Something operational, human reviewed, with no student data in it, so the first thing the county says publicly about its own AI use is modest and verifiable.
  • Stand up a plain, current public page: what is approved, what is not, who to ask, and how to report a problem. Most of the questions I would otherwise answer one at a time live on that page.
  • Report back to the task force and to the people who took the survey, including what did not work.

What exists at day 90: published version one guidance, delivered sessions with feedback data, one measured pilot, a public information page, and a written 6 month plan built from all of it.

What I would deliberately not do in 90 days

  • Not endorse a vendor. An early countywide endorsement locks in a market position before anyone has evidence, and it is very hard to reverse.
  • Not put AI anywhere near a consequential student decision. Not identification, not discipline, not eligibility, not grades. Not in year one.
  • Not promise a countywide policy. Districts adopt their own policies. A county office that writes theirs for them creates a document with no local ownership and no legal standing.
  • Not measure success by adoption rate. If the honest finding is that a use case is not worth it, the system needs to be able to hear that from me.

This plan is written from publicly available information about county office structure and California requirements. It is a proposal, not a commitment on behalf of any agency, and the first thing I would expect is for the listening phase to change it.
